Slutsky 方程式：替代效果與所得效果

價格一變，買的數量就跟著變。Slutsky 把這個變化拆成兩段：因為「相對價格變了」而換著買，和因為「口袋變深或變淺」而多買少買。考題永遠是同一套五個步驟，三種效用函數各練一次就會了。

降價時同時發生的兩件事

咖啡從 2 元降到 1 元。第一件事：咖啡相對茶變便宜了，你會多喝咖啡、少喝茶。第二件事：原本那一籃東西現在不用花那麼多錢，等於口袋裡多出一筆錢，你會拿去多買東西。

第一段 替代效果

英文是 substitution effect。只看「相對價格改變」造成的變化，把購買力多出來的部分先收走。

第二段 所得效果

英文是 income effect。價格不再動，只看「購買力多出來（或少掉）」造成的變化。

$$\underbrace{\Delta x_1}_{\text{需求量的總變化}} \;=\; \underbrace{\Delta x_1^{s}}_{\text{替代效果}} \;+\; \underbrace{\Delta x_1^{n}}_{\text{所得效果}}$$

任何價格變動造成的需求變化，一定可以拆成這兩段相加。

Slutsky 的做法：先把多出來的購買力收走

問題是兩件事同時發生，怎麼分開？Slutsky 的辦法是問一個假想的問題：

「價格已經變成新的了。如果我把你的所得調整到剛剛好買得起原來那一籃，你會怎麼選？」

這條假想的預算線叫補償預算線：斜率是新的價格比，而且通過原本的組合 A。在這條線上選出來的點叫 B。

  • A → B：購買力沒有變（還是剛好買得起 A），只有相對價格變了，所以是純替代效果（pure substitution effect）。
  • B → C：把收走的所得還給他，價格不動，所以是所得效果。C 就是降價後真正的選擇。
$$m' \;=\; p_1'\,x_1^{A} + p_2\,x_2^{A} \;=\; m + (p_1' - p_1)\,x_1^{A}$$

意思是「用新價格買舊組合要花多少錢」。降價時 $m' \lt m$（收走一些），漲價時 $m' \gt m$（補貼一些）。

下面是作業第 1 題的數字：$U = x_1^2x_2$、$m = 45$、$p_2 = 1$，$p_1$ 從 2 降到 1。按步驟一格一格看：

自己調價格看看

同一個人，換不同的新價格。注意橘色虛線永遠穿過 A；漲價時（$p_1' \gt 2$）兩個箭頭都往左。

五個步驟（每一題都一樣）

  1. 原本的組合 A：需求函數代入舊價格和所得 $m$。
  2. 補償所得 $m'$：用新價格買 A 要花多少錢，$m' = m + \Delta p \times (\text{A 裡那個商品的數量})$。
  3. 中間點 B：需求函數代入新價格和 $m'$。
  4. 最終的組合 C：需求函數代入新價格和原來的 $m$。
  5. 相減：替代效果 $= B - A$，所得效果 $= C - B$。檢查兩個加起來等於 $C - A$。
點用哪個價格用哪個所得代表什麼
A舊價格$m$價格變動前的選擇
B新價格$m'$（補償所得）假想的中間點，現實中不會真的發生
C新價格$m$價格變動後的選擇

$m'$ 是用新價格乘舊數量，不是用舊價格。另外替代效果是 $B - A$、所得效果是 $C - B$，順序寫反整題就錯。變的是哪個商品的價格，$\Delta p$ 就乘哪個商品在 A 裡的數量。

所得 100，$p_1$ 從 4 降到 2，原本買了 $x_1 = 10$。補償所得 $m'$ 是多少？

$m' = m + (p_1' - p_1)\,x_1 = 100 + (2 - 4)\times 10 = 80$。降價讓原來那一籃便宜了 20 元，所以要收走 20 元才是「剛好買得起」。

兩個效果的方向：正常財、劣等財、Giffen 財

替代效果：一定讓需求量增加或不變，永遠跟價格反方向。降價就多買，漲價就少買，沒有例外。

所得效果：降價等於實質所得增加。正常財（normal good）所得增加會多買；劣等財（income-inferior good）所得增加反而少買。

財貨類型替代效果所得效果總效果（降價時）需求曲線
正常財 normal＋＋（互相加強）買更多一定負斜率
劣等財 inferior＋−（比較小）還是買更多負斜率
Giffen 財＋−（比較大）買更少正斜率，違反需求法則

所以需求法則（Law of Downward-Sloping Demand）對正常財一定成立。Giffen 財是劣等財裡的極端情況：所得效果大到蓋過替代效果。

為什麼替代效果不可能反方向？

用上一章的顯示性偏好想。補償預算線通過 A，所以線上在 A 左上方的那一段，在原本的預算線底下都買得起，當初卻選了 A，表示那些點都不比 A 好。現在他不會跑去選那一段，只會留在 A 或往變便宜的商品那一側移動。

某商品降價後，替代效果讓需求量 +6，所得效果讓需求量 −2。這個商品是？

降價時所得效果是負的，表示實質所得增加反而少買，是劣等財。但總效果 $6 - 2 = +4$ 還是正的，降價後買更多，需求曲線仍是負斜率，所以不是 Giffen 財。

阿明只吃「一個漢堡配兩包薯條」的套餐，多的漢堡或多的薯條對他都沒用。漢堡漲價後他買的漢堡變少了。減少的部分是？

這是完全互補。不管相對價格怎麼變，他都維持固定搭配，替代效果是 0。漢堡變少只是因為漲價後買得起的套餐數變少了，全部是所得效果。

補充：Hicks 的替代效果

簡報最後一頁問了一句：「在新價格下，要多少所得才能回到原來的效用水準？」這是 Hicks 的拆法。和 Slutsky 只差在「補償到哪裡」：

Slutsky 補償

補償到買得起原來的組合。補償預算線通過 A。作業指定用這一種。

Hicks 補償

補償到回到原來的無異曲線。補償預算線和通過 A 的那條無異曲線相切。

用作業第 1 題的數字比較兩種拆法

原本的效用 $U_0 = 15^2 \times 15 = 3375$。新價格 $(1, 1)$ 下，所得 $m$ 的人會選 $x_1 = \tfrac{2m}{3}$、$x_2 = \tfrac{m}{3}$，效用是 $\tfrac{4m^3}{27}$。令它等於 3375：

$$\frac{4m^3}{27} = 3375 \;\Rightarrow\; m \approx 28.35 \;\Rightarrow\; x_1 \approx 18.90$$
拆法補償所得中間點的 $x_1$替代效果所得效果
Slutsky3020+5+10
Hicks28.3518.90+3.90+11.10

總效果都是 +15，只是切的位置不同。題目寫 Slutsky 就用 Slutsky。

Cobb-Douglas utility: $U = x_1^2x_2$

A consumer has the utility function $U = x_1^2x_2$ and faces the budget constraint $p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = m$.

(A) Derive the demand functions for $x_1$ and $x_2$.
(B) Suppose $p_1 = 2$, $p_2 = 1$ and $m = 45$. Find the optimal consumption bundle.
(C) Now $p_1$ falls from 2 to 1, while $p_2$ and $m$ stay the same. Find the new optimal consumption bundle.
(D) Using the Slutsky equation, find the pure substitution effect and the income effect on $x_1$ of the price change in part (C).

提示（中文）

(A) 用兩個條件：相切 $\text{MRS} = -\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}$，加上預算式。$MU_1 = 2x_1x_2$，$MU_2 = x_1^2$。(D) 先算「用新價格買舊組合要多少錢」。

(A) Tangency condition

At the optimum, the MRS equals the slope of the budget line:

$$\text{MRS} = -\frac{MU_1}{MU_2} = -\frac{2x_1x_2}{x_1^2} = -\frac{2x_2}{x_1} = -\frac{p_1}{p_2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad p_1x_1 = 2\,p_2x_2$$

Spending on good 1 is twice the spending on good 2.

(A) Substitute into the budget constraint

$$p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = m \;\Rightarrow\; 2p_2x_2 + p_2x_2 = m \;\Rightarrow\; x_2^* = \frac{m}{3p_2},\qquad x_1^* = \frac{2m}{3p_1}$$

The consumer spends $\tfrac23$ of income on good 1 and $\tfrac13$ on good 2.

(B) Plug in $p_1 = 2$, $p_2 = 1$, $m = 45$

$$x_1^* = \frac{2\times 45}{3\times 2} = 15,\qquad x_2^* = \frac{45}{3\times 1} = 15$$

The original bundle is $A = (15, 15)$. Check: $2\times 15 + 1\times 15 = 45$ ✓

(C) New price $p_1 = 1$

$$x_1^* = \frac{2\times 45}{3\times 1} = 30,\qquad x_2^* = 15$$

The new bundle is $C = (30, 15)$. The total change in $x_1$ is $30 - 15 = +15$.

(D) Compensated income

Adjust income so that the original bundle $A = (15, 15)$ is just affordable at the new prices $(1, 1)$:

$$m' = 1\times 15 + 1\times 15 = 30 \qquad\big(\text{or } m' = m + \Delta p_1\cdot x_1^A = 45 + (1 - 2)\times 15 = 30\big)$$

(D) Intermediate bundle B

Demand at the new prices with income $m' = 30$:

$$x_1^{B} = \frac{2\times 30}{3\times 1} = 20 \qquad\Big(x_2^{B} = \frac{30}{3} = 10\Big)$$

(D) The two effects

$$\text{Substitution effect} = x_1^B - x_1^A = 20 - 15 = +5$$ $$\text{Income effect} = x_1^C - x_1^B = 30 - 20 = +10$$

Check: $5 + 10 = 15$, which equals the total change ✓. Both effects have the same sign, so good 1 is a normal good.

(A) $x_1 = \dfrac{2m}{3p_1}$, $x_2 = \dfrac{m}{3p_2}$. (B) $(15, 15)$. (C) $(30, 15)$. (D) $m' = 30$; substitution effect $= +5$, income effect $= +10$.

Perfect complements: $U = \min\{x_1, 2x_2\}$

A consumer has the utility function $U = \min\{x_1, 2x_2\}$ and faces the budget constraint $p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = m$.

(A) Derive the demand functions for $x_1$ and $x_2$.
(B) Suppose $p_1 = 1$, $p_2 = 2$ and $m = 48$. Find the optimal consumption bundle.
(C) Now $p_2$ rises from 2 to 4, while $p_1$ and $m$ stay the same. Find the new optimal consumption bundle.
(D) Using the Slutsky equation, find the pure substitution effect and the income effect on $x_2$ of the price change in part (C).

提示（中文）

完全互補不能用微分。最適點一定在 L 型的折點，也就是括號裡兩個東西相等：$x_1 = 2x_2$。注意這一題變的是 $p_2$、問的是 $x_2$，所以 $m' = m + \Delta p_2 \times x_2^{A}$。

(A) Kink condition

With perfect complements, the optimal bundle is at the kink of the L-shaped indifference curve, where the two terms inside the min are equal:

$$x_1 = 2x_2$$

(A) Substitute into the budget constraint

$$p_1(2x_2) + p_2x_2 = m \;\Rightarrow\; x_2^* = \frac{m}{2p_1 + p_2},\qquad x_1^* = \frac{2m}{2p_1 + p_2}$$

One “set” (2 units of good 1 with 1 unit of good 2) costs $2p_1 + p_2$. The consumer buys as many sets as income allows.

(B) Plug in $p_1 = 1$, $p_2 = 2$, $m = 48$

$$x_2^* = \frac{48}{2 + 2} = 12,\qquad x_1^* = 24$$

The original bundle is $A = (24, 12)$. Check: $1\times 24 + 2\times 12 = 48$ ✓

(C) New price $p_2 = 4$

$$x_2^* = \frac{48}{2 + 4} = 8,\qquad x_1^* = 16$$

The new bundle is $C = (16, 8)$. Check: $16 + 4\times 8 = 48$ ✓. The total change in $x_2$ is $8 - 12 = -4$.

(D) Compensated income

Income needed to buy the original bundle $A = (24, 12)$ at the new prices $(1, 4)$:

$$m' = 1\times 24 + 4\times 12 = 72 \qquad\big(\text{or } m' = m + \Delta p_2\cdot x_2^A = 48 + (4 - 2)\times 12 = 72\big)$$

The price went up, so income must be increased to keep A affordable.

(D) Intermediate bundle B

$$x_2^{B} = \frac{72}{2\times 1 + 4} = 12 \qquad\big(x_1^{B} = 24\big)$$

B is the same bundle as A.

(D) The two effects

$$\text{Substitution effect} = x_2^B - x_2^A = 12 - 12 = 0$$ $$\text{Income effect} = x_2^C - x_2^B = 8 - 12 = -4$$

With perfect complements there is no substitution effect. The whole change in $x_2$ is the income effect.

(A) $x_1 = \dfrac{2m}{2p_1 + p_2}$, $x_2 = \dfrac{m}{2p_1 + p_2}$. (B) $(24, 12)$. (C) $(16, 8)$. (D) $m' = 72$; substitution effect $= 0$, income effect $= -4$.

(A) Derive the demand functions for $x_1$ and $x_2$. Discuss the cases according to how the MRS compares with the relative price.
(B) Suppose $p_1 = 3$, $p_2 = 1$ and $m = 30$. Find the optimal consumption bundle.
(C) Now $p_1$ falls from 3 to 1, while $p_2$ and $m$ stay the same. Find the new optimal consumption bundle.
(D) Using the Slutsky equation, find the pure substitution effect and the income effect on $x_1$ of the price change in part (C).

無異曲線是斜率 $-2$ 的直線，預算線斜率是 $-\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}$。兩條直線通常不會相切，最適點會在角落。比較「無異曲線比較陡」還是「預算線比較陡」。

(A) The MRS

$$\text{MRS} = -\frac{MU_1}{MU_2} = -\frac{2}{1} = -2$$

The consumer is always willing to give up 2 units of good 2 for 1 unit of good 1. In the market, 1 unit of good 1 costs $\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}$ units of good 2. So compare $|\text{MRS}| = 2$ with the relative price $\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}$.

(A) Three cases

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} \gt 2$ (that is, $p_1 \gt 2p_2$)Good 1 is too expensive: buy only good 2$0$$\dfrac{m}{p_2}$

The first two cases are corner solutions. Another way to see it: one unit of money spent on good 1 gives utility $\dfrac{2}{p_1}$, and on good 2 gives $\dfrac{1}{p_2}$. The consumer buys only the good with the larger value.

(B) Plug in $p_1 = 3$, $p_2 = 1$, $m = 30$

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = 3 \gt 2$, so good 1 is too expensive and the consumer buys only good 2:

$$A = \Big(0,\ \frac{30}{1}\Big) = (0, 30)$$

(C) New price $p_1 = 1$

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = 1 \lt 2$, so now the consumer buys only good 1:

$$C = \Big(\frac{30}{1},\ 0\Big) = (30, 0)$$

The total change in $x_1$ is $30 - 0 = +30$.

(D) Compensated income

Income needed to buy the original bundle $A = (0, 30)$ at the new prices $(1, 1)$:

$$m' = 1\times 0 + 1\times 30 = 30 \qquad\big(\text{or } m' = m + \Delta p_1\cdot x_1^A = 30 + (1 - 3)\times 0 = 30\big)$$

The consumer bought no good 1 at first, so the price cut does not make the original bundle cheaper. Hence $m' = m$.

(D) Intermediate bundle B

At the new prices $\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = 1 \lt 2$, so with income 30 the consumer buys only good 1: $B = (30, 0)$.

(D) The two effects

$$\text{Substitution effect} = x_1^B - x_1^A = 30 - 0 = +30$$ $$\text{Income effect} = x_1^C - x_1^B = 30 - 30 = 0$$

The whole change in $x_1$ is the substitution effect.
